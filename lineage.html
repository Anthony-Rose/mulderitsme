Lineage

What it inherited, what it passed on

The X-Files sits between the paranoid thrillers of the 1970s and the prestige television that followed it. Scroll the line.

1967

The Invaders

Influence

Quinn Martin's series for ABC: Roy Thinnes as an architect who has seen the aliens and cannot make anyone believe him. Carter cast Thinnes as Jeremiah Smith in 1996.

1974

Kolchak: The Night Stalker

Influence

A reporter who keeps finding monsters nobody believes in. Chris Carter has named it as a direct inspiration.

1974

The Parallax View

Influence

Alan J. Pakula's conspiracy thriller: institutions as a maze, and the hero swallowed by the architecture.

1975

Three Days of the Condor

Influence

Sydney Pollack's CIA thriller ends with Robert Redford claiming he has gone to the press and Cliff Robertson asking how he knows they will print it.

1976

All the President's Men

Influence

Parking-garage informants in the dark. The show's Deep Throat takes his name from Watergate.

1977

Close Encounters of the Third Kind

Influence

Light as the sign of the alien. Every beam that floods a road at night owes it something.

1982

The Thing

Influence

John Carpenter's Antarctic siege: a sealed station, a parasite, and nobody sure who is still human. Glen Morgan and James Wong's Ice borrows the sealed station and the paranoia whole, and the debt is plain on screen.

1990

Twin Peaks

Influence

Proof that network TV could be strange. David Duchovny played DEA agent Denise Bryson.

1991

The Silence of the Lambs

Influence

A young woman in an FBI world of men. Carter has said Scully is partly modeled on Clarice Starling.

1991

JFK

Influence

Oliver Stone's film gives Kevin Costner's Garrison a Pentagon informant named only X, played by Donald Sutherland. When Deep Throat dies, Mulder's next source carries the same letter.

1993

The X-Files

The show

A procedural with a serialized conspiracy underneath. Shot on 35mm, mostly at night, in the rain.

1996

Millennium

Sibling

Carter's darker companion series, about a former FBI profiler who sees what killers see.

1996

Dark Skies

Imitator

NBC's answer, from Bryce Zabel and Brent Friedman: a 1960s UFO cover-up told as alternate history. It lasted one season, the first sign that the formula could be copied but not the hold.

1998

The X-Files (Fight the Future)

The film

Rob Bowman's feature, cast with Martin Landau, Armin Mueller-Stahl and Blythe Danner, bridging seasons five and six.

1999

Harsh Realm

Sibling

Carter's third series, a soldier trapped inside a military simulation. Fox aired three episodes before pulling it, the shortest life of any Ten Thirteen show.

2001

The Lone Gunmen

Sibling

The spinoff about Mulder's three hackers ran thirteen episodes. Its pilot, in which conspirators try to fly a jet into the World Trade Center, aired on March 4.

2004

Lost

Descendant

A serialized mystery that learned from the show's long middle. In 2007 its producers called The X-Files "a cautionary tale" and asked ABC for an end date, which they got.

2005

Supernatural

Descendant

Eric Kripke's series copied the model whole: two investigators, a car, a monster a week, a mythology underneath. David Nutter directed the pilot and Kim Manners produced it until his death in 2009.

2008

Breaking Bad

Descendant

Vince Gilligan's series, with the actor he found in Drive. It kept the X-Files standalone and refused the X-Files mythology.

2008

Fringe

Descendant

An FBI team, a believer and a skeptic, and a weekly case laid over a long mythology.

2008

The X-Files: I Want to Believe

The film

Carter's second feature, shot in a Vancouver winter by Bill Roe, opened a week after The Dark Knight. The one X-Files story that rules for the skeptic.

2016

Stranger Things

Descendant

Government labs, small-town disappearances and flashlights in the woods, filtered through the 1980s.

2019

Evil

Descendant

Robert and Michelle King's series pairs a forensic psychologist with a priest in training and sends them to assess miracles and possessions. The believer-and-skeptic car, with the seats swapped.

2026

The X-Files (Hulu)

The show

Ryan Coogler's pilot, with Danielle Deadwyler and Himesh Patel as new agents, was ordered to series on October 13. The line now has a second core point.

Two parents, not one

The show is usually traced to one source at a time, Kolchak when the subject is monsters and Pakula when the subject is conspiracy, and the two are rarely put in the same sentence. They should be. Kolchak is the television parent: a formula that resets every week, a hero nobody believes, a story spiked before the credits. Twenty episodes of it aired on ABC after two hit television movies, and Carter later gave Darren McGavin two guest spots as a retired agent who had worked the first X-Files. The paranoid thrillers of the 1970s are the film parent, and they end. Frady dies, Woodward and Bernstein win once, Turner may or may not get his story printed. The X-Files took the renewable structure from television and the furniture, the garages and the silhouettes and the informants, from film, and the hybrid is what let it run for nine years. The site's essay on the paranoid style works through the inversion.

Where the look came from

Gordon Willis photographed both Pakula films on the line, and his habit of letting a frame fall off into black is the first thing the show borrowed. Spielberg's Close Encounters supplied the other half of the vocabulary: light from above as the signature of the unexplained. The Pacific Northwest itself was inherited. Twin Peaks shot its pilot in Snoqualmie and North Bend, Washington, and taught networks that fir trees and fog could carry a prime-time hour. Three years later the show found the same weather a few hours north. How the crew turned that weather into a method is the subject of the flashlight essay, and what happened when the weather went away is the subject of the one on the move to Los Angeles.

The siblings

Carter's other series are on the line because they show him reaching for the same device three more times. Millennium gave Lance Henriksen a secret organization whose meaning kept changing, and was rebuilt twice in three seasons. Harsh Realm lasted three broadcast episodes. The Lone Gunmen ran thirteen. None lived long enough to meet the problem the parent show met, which was how to end a story built to be deferred, and the essay on what the show left behind argues that this problem, not the hybrid format, was its real bequest.

What the descendants kept

The writers' room scattered into the next two decades of television, and the line records what each writer carried out. Gilligan took the standalone's discipline to Breaking Bad and left the mythology behind. Howard Gordon went to 24 and co-created Homeland. Frank Spotnitz brought The Man in the High Castle to Amazon and, in 2005, developed ABC's short-lived remake of Night Stalker, closing the loop back to Kolchak. Glen Morgan and James Wong rewrote Jeffrey Reddick's script for Final Destination, a story Reddick had first pitched as an X-Files episode, and Wong directed it. David Nutter shot the Supernatural pilot, which launched the one descendant that adopted the engine whole, with Kim Manners running its Vancouver production until 2009; the Manners essay follows him there. Fringe kept the two tracks and, from its second season, the Vancouver address. Lost kept the serialized mystery and learned to set an end date. Stranger Things kept the flashlights. The Duffer brothers have said they were "big fans" of the show and that its monster-of-the-week episodes "would really scare us growing up." Evil kept the car and the argument inside it.

The second core

Hulu ordered Ryan Coogler's series on October 13, 2026, eight months after the pilot order, with Jennifer Yale as showrunner and Carter credited as an executive producer. The leads are new agents, not Mulder and Scully. If that holds, the franchise is doing in 2026 what its imitators did in 2007: starting over rather than reopening. What a Coogler X-File would look like, and how far his unbroken, crowded frames sit from the original's two people and a beam, is taken up in A Crowd in the Room; the deal itself is sorted in the series-order essay and tracked on the Reboot page.

The truth is out there.
