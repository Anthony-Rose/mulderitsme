Every good investigator needs a source who knows how the machines work. For Mulder, that source was three guys in a cramped office full of monitors, police scanners, and conspiracy newsletters. The Lone Gunmen first showed up in season one's "E.B.E." and quickly became fan favorites.

## Three types of paranoid

The trio works because each one is a different kind of believer. John Fitzgerald Byers is the polite one in the suit who believes in democracy and the rule of law, and is horrified by how often they fail. Melvin Frohike is the gruff veteran with a hopeless crush on Scully. Ringo Langly is the long-haired hacker who handles the computers and the attitude.

Together they publish The Lone Gunman, a newsletter about government secrets. Their office has a door with more locks than anyone needs, and they check every visitor through a camera before they open it. In the early 90s that read as comedy. Today it reads more like basic security hygiene.

## What they got right

The Gunmen were written as a joke about paranoid tech people, but the show also took them seriously. They intercept signals, trace calls, analyze materials, and dig through databases that Mulder can't touch as an FBI agent. They're almost always right about the technical details and often wrong about what those details mean. That's a pretty accurate picture of a lot of real-world research.

Season five's "Unusual Suspects" gives them an origin story, set in 1989, and shows how a regular guy, a cable repairman, and a hacker ended up as a team. It's one of the most charming hours in the run.

## The spinoff

In 2001 the trio got their own series, The Lone Gunmen. It ran for thirteen episodes and leaned into comedy and caper stories. Its pilot, which aired in March 2001, involved a plot by people inside the government to fly a hijacked airliner into the World Trade Center. Months later it became one of the most unsettling coincidences in television history.

The show was canceled after one season, and the characters' story was wrapped up in season nine's "Jump the Shark."

## Their legacy

The Gunmen were some of the first sympathetic hacker characters on mainstream TV. They weren't villains or geniuses in hoodies. They were cranky, loyal, and funny, and they used their skills to protect people. Not a bad model.
