When people picture The X-Files, they usually picture the same few things: a flashlight beam cutting through fog, wet pine trees, a gray sky that never quite turns into daylight. Almost all of that came from one place. The first five seasons were filmed in and around Vancouver, British Columbia.

## A city that can play anywhere

Vancouver stood in for small towns in Oregon, research stations in Alaska, suburbs in Virginia, and woods all over the United States. The production made heavy use of the region's forests, industrial areas, and old buildings, and the constant overcast light did a lot of the atmosphere work for free. You don't need a big lighting budget to make a scene look ominous when the weather is already doing it.

The rain became part of the show's identity. It made night shoots harder on the crew, but it gave every exterior scene a texture that's hard to fake.

## The move

After season five, the production moved to Los Angeles. The reasons reported over the years include David Duchovny wanting to be closer to his family in California and the practical pull of filming nearer the studio. Whatever the mix of reasons, the change is obvious on screen.

Season six is sunnier. Literally. The desert, the bright suburbs, and the clear skies gave the show a different palette, and the writers leaned into it with lighter episodes like "Triangle," "Arcadia," and "Monday." Some of those are among the most inventive hours in the series, but the shift in tone is real.

## Coming back

When the revival came in 2016, production returned to Vancouver. The first shot of rain on a windshield felt like coming home for a lot of fans. It's a reminder that the setting was never just a backdrop. It was part of the mood the show sold you.

## Spot the location

If you rewatch the early seasons, keep an eye out for the same forests, the same industrial lots, and even the same buildings showing up as different places. Once you notice it you can't stop, and it says a lot about how much the crew got out of a limited set of locations.
