summary: The alien conspiracy storyline spans eleven seasons and two movies. Here's a route through it that won't leave you lost or burned out.

The mythology is the long story underneath The X-Files: abductions, colonists, black oil, the Syndicate, and what happened to Samantha Mulder. It's also the part new viewers most often bounce off, because it's spread across more than sixty episodes mixed in with standalone cases. You don't have to watch all of them in order to follow it. You just need a route.

## Stage one: the setup (seasons 1 and 2)

Start with the "Pilot" and "Deep Throat." Together they establish the rules: Mulder believes, Scully measures, and someone powerful is watching both of them. Then jump to "The Erlenmeyer Flask," the season one finale, where the show stops hinting and starts proving.

Season two is built around Scully's abduction. Watch "Duane Barry," "Ascension," and "One Breath" as a set. Finish the season with "Colony," "End Game," and "Anasazi."

## Stage two: the big middle (seasons 3 to 5)

This is the mythology at its most confident. "The Blessing Way" and "Paper Clip" open season three with real momentum. After that, pick up "Nisei" and "731," then "Piper Maru" and "Apocrypha" for the black oil.

Season four's essential hour is "Musings of a Cigarette Smoking Man," which isn't strictly a mythology episode but tells you everything about the man running it. Then watch "Memento Mori," Scully's cancer storyline, and "Gethsemane." Season five's "Redux" two-parter resolves that arc, and "Patient X" and "The Red and the Black" introduce the rebels.

## Stage three: the movie and the payoff

Watch "The End" from season five, then the 1998 film Fight the Future. The movie was written to bridge seasons five and six, and it plays far better in that spot than on its own.

Season six's "Two Fathers" and "One Son" are where the Syndicate's story actually ends. A lot of fans consider this the true conclusion of the original conspiracy, and it's a satisfying place to stop if you want to.

## Stage four: optional

Seasons eight and nine shift the story toward the super soldiers and new agents Doggett and Reyes. It's better than its reputation, especially Robert Patrick's work, but it's a different show. Treat it as a sequel series. The revival seasons from 2016 and 2018 reopen the mythology again, with mixed results.

## One rule

Mix in standalone episodes as you go. The mythology was designed to be watched with breaks between installments, and it gets heavy without them.
