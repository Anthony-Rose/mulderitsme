title: Everybody Has a Camera Now
date: 2026-11-19
tag: Form
hue: 62
summary: Darin Morgan's 2016 episode gives Mulder a phone he cannot work and a proof nobody else will ever see. Two years later Morgan wrote the villain who says what that kind of believing had turned into.
---
Two witnesses have described a creature in the Oregon woods, one giving it three eyes and the other a single eye. Fox Mulder points out that neither of them photographed it, which is odd, he says, because "everybody always has a camera on them these days." He proposes a mountain lion, then gray wolves. *Mulder and Scully Meet the Were-Monster*, the third episode of the 2016 revival, went out on Fox on February 1 of that year. Darin Morgan wrote it, and it was the first X-Files he directed.

Mulder has started the hour in the basement telling Scully that since they went away much of the unexplained has been explained, as fraternity pranks or publicity stunts. "I'm a middle-aged man, Scully," he says. A series whose cases used to close with the proof lost or confiscated had come back into a decade where a witness with no picture is doubted for that alone.

Keith Uhlich, recapping the episode for [Vulture](https://www.vulture.com/2016/02/x-files-recap-season-10-episode-3.html), wrote that age seemed to have "somewhat mellowed Morgan." Mulder does get his faith back. Morgan arranges it so that he gets it as a man with a story and no picture, and two years later, in *The Lost Art of Forehead Sweat*, he wrote a villain for whom that is everybody's condition.

## The camera app

At a truck stop that night there is a noise in the dark. Mulder says he is going to get a good picture, and takes out his phone. Scully says that if the thing looks like the drawings she is emptying her clip into it. The phone has a new camera app and he is not sure it works. Asked whether it is shooting stills or video, he doesn't know. Told to go to Settings, he asks where.

In the autopsy bay Mulder scrolls through the results: an ear that may be a foot, a blur that looks like something if you squint, and a video shot, Scully tells him, with the lens pointed the wrong way. [EatTheCorn](https://www.eatthecorn.com/2016/02/03/10x3-mulder-and-scully-meet-the-were-monster/), the fan-run archive, calls the scene an exact repeat of one in the third season's *Quagmire*. Twenty years on Mulder has a far better camera and the same pictures.

The monster has taken a job selling the device. Guy Mann, played by Rhys Darby in what EatTheCorn identifies as the hat and pale suit of Carl Kolchak, is a lizard who was bitten by a man and now wakes up human every morning, and once he has clothes on, the compulsion that takes him over is to find steady work. He walks into a shop called Smart Phones Is Us and pitches a model with "3,000 gigabertz of pixelbitz." "I have no idea what I'm saying," he tells Mulder, "and neither do my customers." He credits people with one Darwinian advantage over other animals, the ability to BS their way through anything.

The phone shop is new to this version. Morgan wrote the story about ten years earlier for Frank Spotnitz's remake of *The Night Stalker*, which by EatTheCorn's history was canceled before the script could be shot; he told Ben Lindbergh of [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2018/01/24/tv/the-x-files-darin-morgan-q-a) in 2018 that he had wanted to make it for almost a decade. The old script was released as a DVD extra, and the archive, comparing the two, reports that Guy originally went to work in a donut shop and that Mulder's midlife crisis has no counterpart in the Kolchak draft. On that reading, what Morgan added for The X-Files was a phone and the age of the man holding it.

## One actor, both parts

A motel room comes before the cemetery, and in it the show's founding quarrel, believer against skeptic, is played by David Duchovny alone. Mulder announces that the suspect is a monster and then supplies Scully's objections for her, each introduced with a version of "To which I know you're going to say." Among them are her rabies explanation and her diagnosis of him as a lunatic. Gillian Anderson listens, smiles, and answers when he stops: "Yeah, this is how I like my Mulder."

Morgan stages the argument once and moves on to a man talking in a graveyard. Guy's confession takes up, by EatTheCorn's estimate, a quarter to a third of the running time. It replays scenes the episode has already shown, including the portable toilet where somebody took his picture ("That was me, actually," Mulder says), from inside the costume. Mulder is the audience, and he heckles. He is "just looking for some kind of internal logic." Guy wants to know why, since there is no external logic to any of it. Asked point-blank whether he is BSing, the witness says, "I don't know. Maybe."

One passage gets thrown out. Guy describes Scully seducing him in the phone store, and Mulder cuts in: "That did not happen." Guy admits it, explains that since turning human he can't help lying about his sex life, and swears it is the sole untruth in the story. Mulder can strike that scene because he knows Scully. For the rest he has nothing to check against, and his verdict is "it's just silly," which is the word Morgan chose, talking to Lindbergh, for the quest itself: "I always thought it was kind of silly."

Scully closes the case with lab work, and the animal control officer is in custody before Mulder arrives. The one photograph of his that turns out to show anything is of a bite mark, which fits Guy's story at the point where a human bit him. "If Guy's story were true?" Scully repeats.

## The handshake

In the woods, Guy is undressing for a hibernation that Uhlich's recap gives as 10,000 years. Mulder tells him he knows now that the story was true. Guy hears the doubt, and Mulder answers with the sentence on his poster: "I want to believe." They shake hands. Mulder gets as far as the first syllable of "Likewise" before the hand in his belongs to the lizard, which runs. Uhlich has him "slightly shocked and speechless," then smiling, and he finishes the word with nobody left to hear it. There is no phone anywhere in that description.

Mulder, who began the hour suspicious of any witness without a photograph, ends it as one. In 2016 that could pass for a happy ending, since the thing he saw was real and harmed nobody.

## Dr. They

*The Lost Art of Forehead Sweat* aired on January 24, 2018, the fourth of season eleven's ten episodes, with Morgan writing and directing again. He told Lindbergh that *Were-Monster* started the usual way, from "a weird werewolf story," and that this one started from wanting "to write something about Trump." Early on Mulder is back from a day of Bigfoot hunting with his phone turned off. He begins telling Scully about a Sasquatch footprint he found some 35 years ago. She has heard it, and hangs up.

The stranger who then gets Mulder to a parking garage, in what Brian Tallerico's [Vulture](https://www.vulture.com/2018/01/the-x-files-recap-season-11-episode-4.html) recap calls "full Deep Throat mode," stands where Mulder stood in those woods. Reggie, played by Brian Huskey, remembers years of casework beside the two agents and cannot show any of it. "Just prove that I'm real!" he shouts at Scully. Most of the hour is a man telling Mulder a story, and again Scully settles it with paper: a yearbook, a school transcript, a federal employment history that ends at the NSA, where Reggie learned about them by listening to their calls. The Sasquatch conversation is among the ones he is heard monitoring.

Before Scully produces that file, Mulder has met Dr. They, the man Reggie blames for erasing him. They explains that Mulder's time has passed and that the powerful no longer bother keeping secrets, because nobody can tell what is real. Mulder says he isn't believing any of this. "Believe what you want to believe," They answers. "That's what everybody does nowadays anyway." The sentence on Mulder's poster, last spoken as a courtesy to a lizard, comes back from They reworded as a description of the country. They goes on to concede that he cannot control minds. It is enough that some people think he might, and for that, "All you really need is a laptop." Morgan told Lindbergh that the quest he once mocked now seemed to him "sentimental and sad."

At the end Mulder has found his lost episode on a knockoff show called *The Dusky Realm*, and Scully has made Goop-O A-B-C, the three-layer gelatin dessert she remembered from childhood. She had nothing to set it in, she says, "so I used your Bigfoot impression as a mold." Kimberly Roots's recap at [TVLine](https://www.tvline.com/interviews/the-x-files-recap-season-11-episode-4-the-lost-art-of-forehead-sweat-darin-morgan-908340/) notes that she decides not to eat it. "I want to remember how it all was," Scully says. Lindbergh asked Morgan about the line. A lot of things, he said, and "I'd rather not specify."
