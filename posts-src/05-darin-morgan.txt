title: The Four Episodes That Taught the Show to Laugh

tag: Writers

summary: Darin Morgan wrote just four episodes in the original run, and they changed what The X-Files thought it could be.
---
For its first season and a half, The X-Files was almost entirely serious. Then a writer named Darin Morgan, the brother of longtime show writer Glen Morgan, turned in a script about a murder in a town full of retired circus performers. The show was never quite the same.

## "Humbug" (season 2)

"Humbug" is the first X-Files episode that's openly funny, and it's funny about the show itself. Mulder and Scully investigate a killing in a Florida community of sideshow performers, and the episode keeps poking at the idea of who counts as a "freak." It's a comedy with a real monster and a real question at its center.

## "Clyde Bruckman's Final Repose" (season 3)

This is the one people point to as the best episode of the series. Peter Boyle plays an insurance salesman who can see how people are going to die, and he's miserable about it. It's funny, then it's sad, and then it's both at once. Morgan won an Emmy for the script, and Boyle won one for his performance.

## "War of the Coprophages" (season 3)

A town panics about killer cockroaches. Mulder investigates alone and keeps calling Scully, who is at home trying to have a quiet evening. It's the lightest of the four, but it's sharp about mass panic and how fast a rumor becomes a fact.

## "Jose Chung's From Outer Space" (season 3)

An alien abduction told through a novelist's interviews, where every witness contradicts the others. Men in black, an alien who is himself abducted, and a Mulder seen through the eyes of people who find him strange. It's the show taking its own mythology apart and somehow making you love it more.

## Why they matter

Before Morgan, the show's tone had one setting. After him, the writers knew that an X-File could be a comedy, a tragedy, or a satire, and still be an X-File. Later highlights like "Bad Blood," "Small Potatoes," and "The Post-Modern Prometheus" probably don't happen without these four.

Morgan returned for the revival and wrote two more: "Mulder and Scully Meet the Were-Monster" in 2016 and "The Lost Art of Forehead Sweat" in 2018. Both are worth your time, and both sound exactly like him.
