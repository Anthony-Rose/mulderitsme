Season five's "Bad Blood" opens with Mulder driving a wooden stake into a teenager in the woods. Then it rewinds. The rest of the episode is Mulder and Scully telling the story of how they got there, one version at a time, while they wait to find out whether Mulder just killed a vampire or an ordinary kid with fake teeth.

## Two versions of the same night

The structure is borrowed from Rashomon, but the joke is personal. In Scully's version, Mulder is a hyperactive, rude know-it-all who drags her across Texas on a whim. In Mulder's version, Scully is a cold, sarcastic grump who won't take anything seriously. Both versions are a little true, and both are deeply unfair.

The details change between tellings in ways that reward close attention: who orders the pizza, how the sheriff smiles, what exactly everyone said. It's an episode you want to rewatch just to catch them.

## The sheriff

Luke Wilson plays the local sheriff, and he's played differently in each version. To Scully he's a charming, good-looking lawman. To Mulder he's a buck-toothed rube. It's a small joke that tells you exactly what each agent is paying attention to.

## Written by Vince Gilligan

The script is by Vince Gilligan, who wrote and produced for the show for years before he created Breaking Bad. You can see his love for precise, escalating comic setups here. He also knows when to stop joking. The ending, which we won't spoil, is a perfect final beat.

## Why it works

"Bad Blood" only works because the show had five seasons of these characters behind it. You need to know the "real" Mulder and Scully to enjoy how badly they misremember each other. It's a comedy about partnership: two people who would die for each other and still find each other completely exhausting.

## If you liked it

Try "Small Potatoes" from season four, another Gilligan script, where a shapeshifter takes Mulder's place and discovers his life is a lot less glamorous than you'd expect.
