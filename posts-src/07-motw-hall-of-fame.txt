---
The mythology gets the headlines, but the standalone episodes are where most fans fell in love. Here are ten of the best monsters the show ever put on screen, ranked by how badly they ruined our sleep.

## 10. The Peacock family ("Home," season 4)

A farmhouse in a quiet Pennsylvania town and a family that has kept to itself for a very long time. The episode was so disturbing that it reportedly didn't rerun on Fox for years. It earns its place on reputation alone.

## 9. Leonard Betts ("Leonard Betts," season 4)

An EMT who survives being decapitated and has an unusual relationship with cancer. A gross-out premise that turns into one of the show's most important moments for Scully.

## 8. The Flukeman ("The Host," season 2)

Half man, half parasitic flatworm, living in the sewers of New Jersey. It's the first monster many kids of the 90s remember, and it's the reason a lot of us still hesitate before using a portable toilet.

## 7. Eve 6 and Eve 8 ("Eve," season 1)

Identical little girls from a genetics experiment who are much smarter, and much more dangerous, than they look. Creepy kids done right.

## 6. Donnie Pfaster ("Irresistible," season 2)

No supernatural powers to speak of, at least at first. A human predator who targets Scully, and one of the few episodes where the show deals directly with trauma. It's horror because it's so plausible.

## 5. The ice worm ("Ice," season 1)

A parasite pulled up from deep in the Arctic ice, and a small group of people who can't tell who's carrying it. A clear nod to The Thing and a showcase for the early show's claustrophobia.

## 4. Robert Patrick Modell ("Pusher," season 3)

A man who can make people do anything just by talking to them. The game of Russian roulette at the end is one of the tensest scenes in the series.

## 3. The fungus ("Field Trip," season 6)

Mulder and Scully wake up from a case that may never have ended. A mind-bending story about what's real, with a monster that's literally under your feet.

## 2. The man in the car ("Drive," season 6)

Not a creature at all, but a man trapped in a nightmare. Bryan Cranston's performance is the reason Vince Gilligan later cast him in a very different show.

## 1. Eugene Victor Tooms ("Squeeze" and "Tooms," season 1)

Still the gold standard. Every air vent you've ever looked at sideways is because of him.
