CASE FILE 09 // REVIVAL

The Revival Seasons, Honestly

Filed July 10, 20262 min readBy the Basement Desk
DECLASSIFIED

Seasons 10 and 11 brought Mulder and Scully back after fourteen years. Some of it doesn't work. Some of it is the show at its very best.

In 2016, fourteen years after the original finale, The X-Files came back for a six-episode tenth season. A ten-episode eleventh season followed in 2018. Fan opinion on the revival is split, and both sides have a point. Here's an honest guide to what's worth watching.

The mythology episodes

The revival bookends both seasons with mythology episodes, and these are the weakest part. "My Struggle" and its sequels try to rewrite a lot of the original conspiracy, and the result is crowded and rushed. If the old mythology matters a lot to you, you may want to treat them as optional.

The standalones are great

The good news is that the revival's standalone episodes are often excellent. They feel like the old show with better cameras.

"Mulder and Scully Meet the Were-Monster" (season 10) is the highlight. Darin Morgan's script is funny, strange, and kind, and it plays with the idea that Mulder is getting older and wondering whether any of it mattered.

"Home Again" (season 10) is a dark story about homelessness and a monster made of garbage, paired with a moving storyline about Scully's mother.

"The Lost Art of Forehead Sweat" (season 11) is Morgan again, this time about memory and the feeling that your past might have been misremembered.

"Rm9sbG93ZXJz" (season 11) is almost wordless. Mulder and Scully go to a sushi restaurant run entirely by automated systems, and things go badly when they don't leave a tip. The title is the word "Followers" encoded in base64, a nice touch for anyone who works with computers.

"Familiar" (season 11) is a solid creepy small-town horror episode in the classic style.

What the revival gets right

David Duchovny and Gillian Anderson slip back into these characters as if they never left. The show is smart about letting them be older and more tired, and about how the world has changed: when everyone carries a camera in their pocket, a lot of mysteries look different.

Should you watch it

Yes, selectively. Watch the standalone episodes listed above, then decide whether the mythology episodes are worth it to you. And if you enjoy the show's comedy, Morgan's two revival scripts belong right next to his originals.
