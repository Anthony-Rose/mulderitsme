CASE FILE 03 // CHARACTERS

Scully's Skepticism Was the Show's Secret Weapon

Filed January 9, 20262 min readBy the Basement Desk
DECLASSIFIED

Mulder gets the theories, but Scully is the reason any of it is believable. A look at the character who made doubt heroic.

It's easy to describe The X-Files as Mulder's show. He has the obsession, the tragic backstory, and most of the "I told you so" moments. But the series doesn't work without Dana Scully, and it took a lot of viewers years to notice how much of the weight she was carrying.

Doubt as a skill

Scully was assigned to the X-Files to debunk them. The interesting choice the writers made was that she never stopped doing that job, even after she believed in her partner. She keeps asking for evidence. She performs the autopsy. She writes the report that the Bureau will read. Her skepticism isn't a character flaw to be cured by the finale. It's a skill, and it's often the thing that saves the case.

That's also why the show's best scares land. When Scully can't explain something, you know it's serious, because she tried.

Faith and science in one person

The writers gave her a contradiction that made her more interesting than a simple rationalist: she's a scientist and a practicing Catholic. Episodes like "Revelations" and "All Souls" put those two sides of her in conflict, and she doesn't resolve it neatly. She ends up open to miracles in the church and closed to them in the morgue, which is a very human way to live.

The Scully effect

Over the years a lot of women working in science, medicine, and law enforcement have said the character influenced their career choices. The idea became known as "the Scully effect," and a 2018 report from the Geena Davis Institute on Gender in Media looked into it. It found that many women in STEM fields who watched the show credited Scully with raising their interest in science. Fiction rarely gets to point at something that concrete.

Gillian Anderson's work

None of this works without the performance. Gillian Anderson plays Scully as someone who is tired, precise, funny when you least expect it, and fiercely protective of the people she cares about. Watch her in "Memento Mori" or "Irresistible" and then in a comedy like "Bad Blood." It's the same person in every scene, which is harder than it looks.

The point

Mulder wanted to believe. Scully wanted to know. The show was smart enough to make both of those feel heroic.
