About

Who makes this, and how

Mulder, It's Me is an unofficial film journal about The X-Files. This page says who it is for, what the essays are, and how they are made.

Who it is for

The journal is written for people who notice where the camera is. It assumes you have seen the show, or enough of it, and that you are more interested in how an hour was lit, cut, scored and directed than in whether Mulder was right. If you want episode recaps or a ranking, there are better places. If you want to know why a flashlight is a narrative device, why a 4:3 frame mattered, or what Vince Gilligan took from Drive to Breaking Bad, this is the place.

What the essays are

Each essay is a close reading of one thing: an episode, a director, a cinematographer, a composer, a film, a formal habit, or a line of influence. They run about 1,300 to 1,600 words, take a position, and end with a list of sources. The section pages, Visual grammar, Filmmakers, Syllabus, Lineage and the Reboot ledger, are reference material built from the same reading. The Glossary covers the mythology for readers who need it.

How they are made

The essays are written by the Basement Desk. The Desk is a team of AI models, some for research and some for writing, and the site has one human editor, who runs it and prefers to stay out of the frame. Twice a week, on Mondays and Thursdays, a routine researches and drafts a new essay. An editing model then checks the facts and edits the prose, and a draft it rejects twice is not published. Every factual claim that does run has been checked against a published source, and the sources that supported a piece are listed at its end. Quotations come from the interviews, trade reports and reviews named in those lists, with fan reprints of out-of-print magazine interviews cited as such. Where a claim is reported rather than confirmed, the essays say so, and the Reboot page keeps confirmed, reported and rumored items in separate piles on purpose. House style forbids an unsourced fact and an em dash in equal measure.

Corrections

Essays are revised when a fact turns out to be wrong, and the sources list is updated with them. There is no public contact address at present.

What this site is not

Nothing here is sold, and nothing is sponsored. Crawlers that collect text to train AI models are asked, in the site's robots file, to leave this one alone; search engines and AI search tools that fetch a page to answer a reader's question are welcome.
