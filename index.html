    • Series order (confirmed): Hulu picked up Ryan Coogler's The X-Files to series on October 13, 2026, eight months after ordering the pilot. Onyx Collective and 20th Television produce with Proximity Media.
    • Showrunner (confirmed): Jennifer Yale (Legion, See, The Copenhagen Test) is showrunner and executive producer, in place since the February pilot order.
    • Coogler's role (confirmed): Coogler wrote, directed and executive produced the pilot. On the series he is an executive producer, not the day to day showrunner.
    • Leads (confirmed): Danielle Deadwyler and Himesh Patel star as two decorated, mismatched FBI agents assigned to a long shuttered division. Both are co-executive producers.
    • New characters (confirmed): Patel said on the record in July that he and Deadwyler play "completely new characters," and Variety's source agrees. Hulu's announcement does not name the roles.
    • Score (confirmed): Ludwig Göransson scored the pilot, per the Proximity founders' statement. Nothing has been said about who scores the rest of the season.
    • Cinematography (confirmed): Autumn Durald Arkapaw, Coogler's Oscar winning Sinners cinematographer, shot the pilot and posted photos from the Vancouver set. What she shot on has not been stated.
    • 65mm and runtime (rumored): The claim that the pilot runs about two hours and was shot on 65mm film comes from the blog World of Reel, citing unnamed sources. No trade or Hulu confirmation.
    • Mulder and Scully cameo (rumored): World of Reel's October 13 post says Duchovny and Anderson appear in the pilot, attributed only to what the writer has heard. Neither actor nor Hulu has said so.
    • Pilot shoot (reported): Vancouver production listings put the pilot shoot at May 11 to June 11, 2026, and Patel confirmed the wrap in early July. No dates for the rest of the season have been announced.
    • Guest cast (reported): Deadline reported in May that Amy Madigan, Steve Buscemi, Ben Foster, Devery Jacobs, Lochlyn Munro, Tantoo Cardinal, Joel D. Montgrand and Sofia Grace Clifton have guest roles in the pilot.
    • Chris Carter (confirmed): Carter is an executive producer in credit only. He told The Hollywood Reporter in August he had done "exactly nothing" and had chosen not to read the script.
    • Duchovny and Anderson (confirmed): Anderson said in March she had read the pilot script and liked it. Duchovny said in April there was "nothing concrete" and he did not know whether Mulder exists in the show. No casting announced.
    • Premiere (confirmed): No premiere date and no episode count. TheWrap's fall 2027 was its own labeled guess; the season still has to be shot.

    03 · Visual grammar

    How the show saw

    Network television in 1993 was mostly bright, flat and evenly lit so it would read on a small screen. The X-Files went the other way, and most of what people mean by its look comes down to six habits.

    I · DARKNESS

    Black as a working color

    Large parts of the frame are allowed to fall off into true black. The audience fills that space with dread, and the budget never has to show a monster it can't afford.

    II · THE BEAM

    The flashlight as a second camera

    Handheld flashlights are the key light in hundreds of scenes. Wherever an agent points the beam is wherever the audience gets to look, which makes the lighting plan and the point of view one and the same.

    III · ATMOSPHERE

    Haze and hard backlight

    Smoke in the air turns every light source into a visible shaft. Figures are often rimmed from behind and left unlit from the front, reading as silhouettes before they read as people.

    IV · PLACE

    Weather as production design

    The Pacific Northwest supplied overcast skies, wet asphalt and forests that swallow light. The flat gray daylight became as recognizable as the night work.

    V · THE INSERT

    Evidence in close-up

    Slides, implants, bite marks, dental x-rays. The show cuts to objects the way a procedural cuts to clues, and the inserts carry a lot of the storytelling that dialogue doesn't.

    VI · OFF-SCREEN

    Sound beyond the frame

    Mark Snow's synthesized drones and the sound design work in the space the image refuses to show. A low tone tells you something is in the room before the camera does.

    04 · Filmmakers

    Behind the camera

    Television gets remembered as a writer's medium. This show also belongs to a small group of directors, cinematographers and one composer, who between them gave it the feel of a feature film on a weekly schedule.

    Chris Carter

    Creator · writer · director

    Created the series in 1993 and wrote much of its mythology. As a director he favored formal experiments: the black-and-white Post-Modern Prometheus and the long-take Triangle. He later directed the 2008 feature I Want to Believe.

    Kim Manners

    Director · producer

    The show's workhorse, with more than fifty episodes to his name, among them Humbug, Home, Monday and Field Trip. Known on set for pushing the scale of a television day as far as it would go. He died in January 2009.

    Rob Bowman

    Director

    Directed 33 episodes between 1994 and 2000, including Paper Clip, Pusher, Jose Chung's From Outer Space, Kill Switch and Drive, then took the series to cinemas with the 1998 feature.

    David Nutter

    Director

    Shaped the early seasons with Ice, Beyond the Sea and Clyde Bruckman's Final Repose. His gift for performance and tension in small spaces set the template for the standalone episode.

    Vince Gilligan

    Writer · producer

    Wrote Pusher, Bad Blood, Drive and X-Cops, and co-wrote Monday. Bryan Cranston's performance in Drive is the reason Gilligan cast him in Breaking Bad.

    Darin Morgan

    Writer · director

    Four scripts in the original run, Humbug, Clyde Bruckman, War of the Coprophages and Jose Chung, taught the show it could be funny and sad at once. He returned to write and direct Mulder and Scully Meet the Were-Monster.

    Glen Morgan & James Wong

    Writers · directors

    The early-season team behind Squeeze, Ice and Home. Wong directed Morgan's Musings of a Cigarette Smoking Man; decades later Morgan directed the near-silent Rm9sbG93ZXJz.

    The cinematographers

    Thomas Del Ruth · John S. Bartley · Joel Ransom · Bill Roe

    Del Ruth shot the pilot. Bartley, who died in 2025, defined the Vancouver look and won an Emmy for Grotesque. Ransom shot The Post-Modern Prometheus. Bill Roe took over in Los Angeles, won ASC awards for Drive and Agua Mala, and shot I Want to Believe.

    Mark Snow

    Composer

    Scored the series across nine seasons and the revival, almost entirely with synthesizers, plus both films. His theme reached No. 2 on the UK singles chart in 1996, an unlikely place for a whistled melody and a delay pedal's worth of echo. He died in July 2025.

    05 · Syllabus

    Sixteen hours of television as cinema

    Less a "best of" list than a reading list for anyone who cares about form: the episodes that took a risk with structure, image or sound, and the director who carried each one.

    EpisodeTitleDirectorWhy it matters
    1x01PilotRobert MandelThe visual rules arrive fully formed: night exteriors, flashlight keys, rain, photographed by Thomas Del Ruth.
    1x08IceDavid NutterA single Arctic location and five people who can't trust each other. Paranoia staged entirely through blocking and eyelines.
    1x13Beyond the SeaDavid NutterThe first episode built around Scully's interior life, and a lesson in letting a guest performance (Brad Dourif) carry the frame.
    2x05Duane BarryChris CarterAn abduction rendered almost entirely in light and sound. The show working out how to film something it had no intention of showing.
    3x04Clyde Bruckman's Final ReposeDavid NutterComedy and grief held in the same shot. Darin Morgan's script and Peter Boyle's performance are the textbook on tonal control.
    3x20Jose Chung's From Outer SpaceRob BowmanNested, contradictory flashbacks. A satire of how stories get told, told through the grammar it is mocking.
    4x02HomeKim MannersRural horror shot with the patience of a feature. The first episode of the series to carry a viewer discretion warning.
    4x07Musings of a Cigarette Smoking ManJames WongA secret history of postwar America seen from behind the curtain. The villain gets an essay film of his own.
    5x05The Post-Modern PrometheusChris CarterBlack-and-white, an open homage to James Whale's Frankenstein. Joel Ransom's photography was Emmy-nominated.
    5x12Bad BloodCliff BoleTwo incompatible versions of the same night. Unreliable narration used for character comedy, with every detail shifting between tellings.
    6x02DriveRob BowmanA chamber piece in a moving car. Bill Roe won an ASC award for it, and Bryan Cranston's work here led to Breaking Bad.
    6x03TriangleChris CarterBuilt to play as four unbroken takes in the spirit of Hitchcock's Rope, with split-screen in the final acts.
    6x19The UnnaturalDavid DuchovnyDuchovny's first episode as writer and director: a baseball fable set in 1947, and an Emmy-nominated showcase for Bill Roe.
    7x12X-CopsMichael WatkinsShot entirely on videotape in the format of COPS. The monster is barely seen, because the format won't allow it.
    7x17all thingsGillian AndersonAnderson's only episode as writer and director: a slow, meditative Scully story about chance, faith and attention.
    10x03Mulder and Scully Meet the Were-MonsterDarin MorganThe revival's high point: a comedy about middle age and meaning that is also, quietly, about the original run.
    06 · Lineage

    What it inherited, what it passed on

    The X-Files sits between the paranoid thrillers of the 1970s and the prestige television that followed it. Scroll the line.

    1974

    Kolchak: The Night Stalker

    Influence

    A reporter who keeps finding monsters nobody believes in. Chris Carter has named it as a direct inspiration.

    1974

    The Parallax View

    Influence

    Alan J. Pakula's conspiracy thriller: institutions as a maze, and the hero swallowed by the architecture.

    1976

    All the President's Men

    Influence

    Parking-garage informants in the dark. The show's Deep Throat takes his name from Watergate.

    1977

    Close Encounters of the Third Kind

    Influence

    Light as the sign of the alien. Every beam that floods a road at night owes it something.

    1990

    Twin Peaks

    Influence

    Proof that network TV could be strange. David Duchovny played DEA agent Denise Bryson.

    1991

    The Silence of the Lambs

    Influence

    A young woman in an FBI world of men. Carter has said Scully is partly modeled on Clarice Starling.

    1993

    The X-Files

    The show

    A procedural with a serialized conspiracy underneath. Shot on 35mm, mostly at night, in the rain.

    1996

    Millennium

    Sibling

    Carter's darker companion series, about a former FBI profiler who sees what killers see.

    1998

    The X-Files (Fight the Future)

    The film

    Rob Bowman's feature, cast with Martin Landau, Armin Mueller-Stahl and Blythe Danner, bridging seasons five and six.

    2008

    Breaking Bad

    Descendant

    Vince Gilligan's series, with the actor he found in Drive.

    2008

    Fringe

    Descendant

    An FBI team, a believer and a skeptic, and a weekly case laid over a long mythology.

    2016

    Stranger Things

    Descendant

    Government labs, small-town disappearances and flashlights in the woods, filtered through the 1980s.
